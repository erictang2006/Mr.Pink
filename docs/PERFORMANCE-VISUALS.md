# Audience performance screens

Select Interstellar or Levels, enable audio, then enter Performance Mode. Both presentations show physical key labels directly on the piano by default, with a minimal Mr Pink song header. The previous section banners and announcements are absent. Existing arrow-key section/measure controls still work; F8 adds elapsed time to the header. Escape exits, Space controls the manual pedal, and F9 remains emergency reset.

Interstellar uses a restrained blue-white starfield, luminous rising bars, and dim gold assisted notes. The labels follow the active mapping, including the assisted bottom-row bindings. The additional high E receives an F label when the existing octave doubling is enabled in the final section.

Levels uses cyan for left-hand bindings and purple for right-hand bindings. Every pitch in a chord carries its physical trigger labels, including multiple labels where chords share pitches. The physical keycap and piano highlight both follow currently held keys only. The central orb responds to existing note-on events with local elastic deformations: low notes bias left, high notes bias right, and mid-register events can pull in every direction. Chords combine several pulls, and simultaneous hands produce multiple directional extensions. This is driven by note events rather than an audio analyser.

The original physical note durations and shared piano/note-lane geometry remain intact. Pedal-held audio never keeps a released piano key illuminated. Completed bars stop growing on physical release, continue traveling at fixed length, lose their contact glow, and have no audible-sustain extension. The physical highlight set comes from the existing pressed input snapshot; audio sustain ownership remains untouched. No audio or extra notes are generated by this presentation layer.

The existing canvas loop renders the atmosphere, orb, and trails, with device pixel ratio capped at 2 and bounded star/effect counts. Secondary effects add three short-lived contour afterimages, directional sparks with streaks, tip flares, pointed electric forks, edge-local flicker, and activity-driven interior turbulence. Limits are 24 active deformation bursts, 160 sparks, and three afterimages. Effects expire after hits rather than accumulating. A 128-point damped circular spring mesh powers the Levels orb. Strong Gaussian spring forces make broad stretches; separate fast-attack, exponentially narrow burst profiles create pointed protrusions. Hit strength and sharpness increase with chord size and recent note density, with deterministic variation so not every note has the same shape. Computer keys do not provide velocity, so this is musical activity mapping rather than pressure detection. Low notes bias left/lower-left, high notes right/upper-right, and mid notes span all directions. Periodic cubic curves join the rim, and uniform spring expansion is removed so it deforms instead of simply scaling. Outward distortion is bounded to 68% of resting radius, and inward compression to 32%. Its center and resting radius stay fixed. Small bounded simulation steps avoid refresh-rate-dependent jitter and post-stall instability. Reduced motion attenuates deformation and disables particles, flare forks, afterimages, flicker. Piano rendering updates on input, pedal, mapping, and size changes. Practice screens and Roses retain their existing presentations.

Implementation: `PerformanceHeader.tsx`, `PerformanceVisualizer.tsx`, `performanceAtmosphere.ts`, `levelsOrb.ts`, and `performancePiano.ts`, integrated through `App.tsx` and the existing `PianoCanvas.tsx`. Mapping labels and hand colors are derived in `src/piano/performancePresentation.ts`.

Validation: focused presentation, physical-note, and keyboard tests cover fixed released-bar length under pedal, pitch-direction bias, simultaneous orb bulges, spring settling, bounded rapid impulses, and 30/120 Hz consistency. Local browser checks additionally compare piano pixels before/during/after physical release with pedal audio still owned by the audio engine, and measure the actual rendered bar rectangles across frames.

In the updated local 1440×900 headless browser smoke check, a dense chord reached approximately 62% outward deformation and returned to rest. 146 sampled Levels draw callbacks averaged approximately 0.18 ms (maximum 0.40 ms). This is a focused rendering check on this machine, not a guarantee for every display/GPU. Audio engines, input handling, and mapping source files were unchanged.

Outer expanding rings and decorative exterior arcs were removed to simplify the orb. The deforming core, spikes, sparks, tip glow, and short surface trails remain.
