# Roses Intro Builder (bars 1–28)

## Closer chorus audition

The **Roses + Closer** overlay is on by default in Timed Practice and Performance. Its automatic entrance marker is **bar 28, beat 4&**, or **66.900 seconds** from bar 1 at 100 BPM. No manual vocal arm is required for Closer. Existing Roses vocals keep their current bar-4 arm/bar-5 start behavior and remain at full level until you adjust their independent slider or reach the section-end fade. The previous saved default at 20:4& is migrated once to 28:4&, retaining mix/fine-offset settings. Other chosen presets remain available.

The current source is the **246.129604-second, 48 kHz WAV in Downloads**, which differs from the older 261.84-second upload. Local word recognition identified the chorus; inspection of the leading “s” fricative in the spectrogram and 10 ms energy measurements refined its annotation to **48.300 seconds** (about ±20 ms at the soft leading edge). The excerpt starts at **48.180 seconds**, retaining a 120 ms pickup. It therefore starts playing at **66.780 seconds**, placing the annotated word at 66.900. The bundled 32-second excerpt's PCM samples were compared directly with the original and are identical. There is no pitch shift, tempo change, resampling, or offline normalization. Provenance and checksums are in `docs/roses-reference/closer_chorus_alignment.json`.

Choose an **Entrance** preset, then use **Fine offset** to move it in milliseconds (negative = earlier, positive = later). Change timing before entry or restart/loop afterward. **Original Roses / Roses + Closer** lets you compare the overlay; during playback, A/B fades only Closer's bus and keeps its source moving in place. Separate vocal sliders and **Volume fade** control level transitions; adding Closer never automatically fades Roses. Settings persist locally and are shared between Practice and Performance.

For a short audition, select **Timed**, set **100%** speed, choose **25–28**, **21–28**, or **19–28**, prepare the existing backing controls, and press Start. Closer re-enters once per loop. Its raw audio stays at playback rate 1 even when practice speed changes, so use 100% to hear the intended 100 BPM entrance. The original backing and manual Z/X drums are preserved. This is an untransposed audition of the original vocal against the original Roses harmony; no reharmonized backing is introduced. At the unlooped bar-28 section boundary the metronome stops, but an already-playing Closer excerpt continues naturally, rather than cutting off 0.3 seconds after its new entrance. B/Reset still stop it explicitly.

## Stage 10: bars 25–28

At **bar 25 / beat 96 / 57.600 seconds**, an already-armed guitar/build-bass layer automatically repeats without a transport restart or another F6 press. The existing Build Bass instrument plays MIDI 49 (C♯3) for four beats, MIDI 45 (A2) for four beats, then MIDI 40 (E2) for eight beats through bar 28. Bars 1–24 retain their instruments and events. No required kick or clap events are added after bar 20.

The supplied Ableton project confirms unwarped, untransposed guitar audio, with Guitar L panned −0.6 and Guitar R +0.605. The bar-25 repetition uses those L/R positions with the existing WAV and a compensating half-gain on each side. Guitar R ends at beat 112; Guitar L retains its 18.73 ms extra source tail. The earlier guitar renderer remains unchanged. The three-note MIDI, original `.als`, README and rendered reference are retained in `docs/roses-reference/roses_bars25_to28_*`.

Vocal chops have been removed from playback and from Practice/Performance controls. F6 arms only guitar and build bass. Original reference assets remain available for reference. Guitar sources and bass releases finish naturally, and a cued Roses vocal fades over 350 ms at the final section boundary. No new presets, automatic drums or pedal changes are introduced.

The app uses the supplied 100 BPM, 4/4 timing manifests and MIDI in `src/piano/` and `docs/roses-reference/`. The first 12 bars retain their original synth phrase and live drum controls. The new three-track MIDI provides 16 continued intro-pad notes, 24 Break Bass notes, and 24 Break Sub notes for bars 13–20. The bass pitches are C♯3, A2, E2, E2 over bars 13–16, then repeat in bars 17–20. Bass and sub share attack positions, but their final attacks have different durations. The source MIDI is checked by the project tests.

## Play and rehearse

1. Run `npm run dev` locally, select **Roses**, open Sandbox, then choose **Step** or **Timed**. Enable audio.
2. Bars 1–4: play **T** (E4) on each downbeat and **U** (G♯4) or **Y** (F♯4) at beat 2.5. Press **V** near bar 4's end to arm the synth loop for bar 5.
3. From bar 5, play every kick with **Z**. From bar 9, add clap with **X**. These samples start on physical keydown; the practice grid never plays them.
4. Before bar 13, press **N** to arm Break Bass and Break Sub together. Both start on the bar-13 downbeat. If you miss it, the display says **MISSED BASS HANDOFF**; pressing N then enters them at the next bar boundary without restarting the synth. Pressing N twice does not duplicate the backing.
5. **M** plays the optional vocal-effect sample once. The source suggests the end of bars 12, 16, and 20. **B** immediately stops synth, bass, and vocal backing. Space remains the manual sustain pedal.
   The separate full vocal stem uses **L** by default (changeable to C or G in Practice Mode). Press it during bar 4 to arm the stem for the bar-5 downbeat, **9.60 seconds after bar 1** at 100 BPM. The display reads **NOT ARMED**, **ARMED**, or **PLAYING**. The source offset remains **9.60 seconds** and can be adjusted in Practice Mode; the first substantial vocal is around 10.8–10.9 seconds in the original file, so it reaches approximately bar 5 beat 3. The stem continues through bars 21–28 and fades at the final boundary, or stops when backing is stopped/reset.
6. Before bar 21, press **F6** (or **F6 · Build**) to arm the new backing. On the bar-21 downbeat, the old synth/break bass/sub stop and the supplied guitar WAV and MIDI-driven Build Bass begin. The cued full vocal stem continues. Z/X remain manual; there are no required source drum hits in bars 21–24.
7. For section rehearsal, choose **13–16**, **17–20**, **21–24**, or **1–24** in Practice Mode. Arm V/N for earlier sections or F6 for the build before Start. Timed section loops repeat automatically; Step Mode pauses at individual drum targets and returns to the section's first bar at its end.

The sheet shows separate synth, bass, sub, kick, clap, and optional accent lanes. Correct drum targets turn green; missed targets dim, and early/late physical hits are marked at their played position. Bar 20 has no kick targets because the source timeline omits them; Z still plays a kick if you choose to press it.

Fullscreen Performance Mode uses V/N/F6/Z/X/M/B and shows the current bar, backing status, next action, and tempo. Automatic synth, bass, and sub notes do not appear as physically pressed piano notes. The unlooped Roses arrangement finishes after bar 28, allowing audio releases and the newly entering Closer excerpt to continue.

The large Practice Mode beat display reads the existing Tone.Transport clock. It shows four beats, progress to the next beat, and upcoming Z/X targets from the imported rhythm. The optional Performance Mode beat HUD has its own show/hide button. The Audio Calibration panel displays browser context latency estimates, a visual lead control, a grading offset, an optional scheduled click, and an eight-tap Enter/button test. Calibration settings are stored locally per detected output ID (or system default when the browser cannot identify it). Positive grading offset treats a later input as on time. Neither offset changes live drum audio timing or removes hardware latency.

## Sound and source limits

The original remake uses Sylenth1 for Break Bass and Nexus for Break Sub. The pack has no isolated bass/sub audio stems or browser-loadable versions of those plugin patches. This project uses a local Tone.js approximation: a short filtered saw-based bass and a smooth sine sub. Their separate volume sliders are deliberately conservative; adjust them in Practice Mode for the room. The full-mix reference was inspected, but isolated timbre matching or a by-ear audition was not possible here.

The supplied kick and clap WAVs are byte-identical to those already in the project. The optional `woo weird sample roses.wav` is a physical one-shot; its natural tail can extend beyond bar 20, as in the reference. The original 0.8-second experimental E4 sample remains an alternative instrument, though it cannot sustain the score's 2.4-second E4 notes cleanly. No full-mix recording is used as a live instrument or backing track.

`public/roses/roses_vocals_original_timeline.wav` is a byte-for-byte copy of the supplied vocal WAV, not a trim or pitch-shifted version. Its waveform correlates best with the original Roses MP3 at a 0 ms shift in a local sample comparison; the 9.60-second musical offset remains an ear-adjustable estimate relative to this application's remake. The vocal stem is large (38 MB), so allow it to show **READY** before arming. Live drums use preloaded `Tone.Players` and `Tone.immediate()` through the shared output volume; no transport quantization or effects are applied to their keydown path. Chrome checks can verify scheduling and display state, but physical output latency requires testing on the actual MacBook/audio device.

## Free live chords

For extra chord testing, **7** plays A major voiced **A3–C♯4–E4–A4** (57, 61, 64, 69), and **8** plays E major voiced **E4–G♯4–B4–E5** (64, 68, 71, 76). Both use the same EDM stab instrument as 1–6, with 7 balanced at the lower-octave volume. In Roses these two shortcuts replace the individual notes formerly on 7 and 8; the other songs keep their mappings.

In Roses, hold **1** for **D♭add9** (61, 65, 68, 75), **2** for **E♭ major** (63, 67, 70, 75), or **3** for **Fm7** (65, 68, 72, 75). **4** and **5** play D♭add9 (49, 53, 56, 63) and E♭ major (51, 55, 58, 63) one octave lower. **8** plays C♯ minor (61, 64, 68, 73), **7** plays B major (59, 63, 66, 71), and **6** plays A major (57, 61, 64, 69), each with its root doubled at the octave. In Roses only, 4–8 replace their former individual-note shortcuts. J/K remain individual melody notes; L remains the vocal cue. These eight keys default to a dedicated three-layer Roses drop synth: a dark stereo saw body, a short punch texture, and mono root bass. Sandbox → Live chord sound provides six sound controls plus A/B comparison with the previous stab. See [drop sound design](ROSES-DROP-SYNTH.md). The opening melody and automatic synth loop keep their selected instrument. All pitches of a chord attack at the same audio time and work independently of playback, bars, speed, or transpose. Release and press again to retrigger. Existing sustain-pedal behavior applies. Chords illuminate every corresponding piano key and create live note trails in Practice, Sandbox, and Performance; they do not count as practice answers or change backing/drums. Blur, switching views/songs, and F9 release held chords safely.

The live chord instrument adds one separate mono triangle bass root per press. Keys 1/4/8 use C♯2, 2/5 use E♭2, 3 uses F2, 7 uses B2, and 6 uses A2. The bass bypasses chord reverb and follows the most recently pressed chord, returning to a still-held root on release. Space sustains both layers. This root layer is audible only; the existing physical chord highlights and trails retain their original pitches.
